# Style guide — what makes this format work

Breakdown of the reference reel (Instagram, 9:16, ~43 s, 30 fps, "5 Claudes roast your LinkedIn").
Use this when tuning a video or deciding which scene type fits a beat.

## Look
- **Miniature diorama / game-show set**: chunky clay-like block mascots, wooden desks, a stage with
  red curtains, or a navy wall full of tiny framed posters. Warm key light, dark floor, vignette,
  film grain. It should feel like a tiny physical set filmed with a real camera, not flat motion graphics.
- **One color family for the cast** (terracotta/orange clay) — individuality comes from hats and
  accessories (frosting top with sprinkles, cap, headset, beanie, glasses, tie, bowtie, mustache).
- **The "product" is a physical prop**: the thing being improved (a LinkedIn profile, a website,
  a resume, a store page) appears as a card lying on the desk that gets stamped, burned,
  highlighted, rewritten.

## Typography (three layers, always the same positions)
1. **Taped paper banner** at the top — ALL CAPS heavy sans (Archivo Black), cream paper, two strips
   of masking tape. 2–5 words. It is the *chapter title* of the beat, not the voiceover.
   Banners repeat across 2–3 consecutive scenes when one idea spans several shots.
2. **Counter badge** under the banner on the right — a scoreboard: `×4`, `OPINIONS 5`, `KEYWORDS 2`,
   `FEE $0`. Numbers tick up as things appear. Green for good/money, red for multipliers.
3. **Captions**, lower third — one word per torn scrap of terracotta paper, chunky serif (Fraunces 900),
   max 3 words visible, each word pops in when spoken, slight random tilt.

## Pacing
- Cut every **2.5–4.5 s**. Each cut punches in (scale 1.08 → 1) with a tiny shake and white flash,
  then the camera slowly pushes in.
- Something must move every second: characters drop in, signs pop up, a counter ticks,
  a pencil writes, confetti bursts, a cursor clicks.
- Voiceover is fast and conversational (~2.7 words/s). No dead air.

## Narrative arc (10–12 scenes)
| # | Beat | Typical scene type | Banner example |
|---|------|--------------------|----------------|
| 1 | Hook: what it is, with the command / product name | `hero` (+ `command`) | 5 CLAUDES ROAST YOUR LINKEDIN |
| 2 | Mechanism: how it works (multiple agents/perspectives) | `lineup` | FIVE READERS, FIVE VIEWS |
| 3 | The pain, made funny | `before-after` with `rewrite:false` | THE HEADLINE GETS ROASTED |
| 4 | Concrete output #1 | `lineup` with `signs` + auto counter | FIVE HONEST OPINIONS |
| 5 | Transformation | `before-after` | EVERY EXPERIENCE REWRITTEN |
| 6 | Concrete output #2 (detail close-up) | `card` with `highlights` | UP THE SEARCH RESULTS |
| 7 | Emotional payoff | `spotlight` | GET NOTICED |
| 8 | Social proof / result | `list` | MORE CONNECTION REQUESTS |
| 9 | Bonus feature (optional) | `card` / `before-after` | AI HEADSHOT AND BANNER |
| 10 | Price objection killer | `gift` + counter `FEE $0` | FREE TO TEST |
| 11 | Ease ("one link sets it up") | `hero` / `gift` | ONE LINK SETS IT UP |
| 12 | CTA: comment keyword | `cta` | COMMENT LINK FOR THE LINK |

## Caption copy rules
- Write how people talk. Short words. One idea per scene.
- Every scene's `text` should be readable in its own duration (≈ 2.7 words/s).
- Put concrete nouns and numbers in the voiceover ("five agents", "three keywords", "zero dollars").
- The CTA always names a single comment keyword in caps (LINK, ROAST, GUIDE, PROMPT…).

## Instagram/TikTok caption (post text) pattern from the reference
`Comment "LINK" and I'll DM you the link to set up this free <thing> in <tool>... and test it with your own <object>. Comment "LINK" for the link. #hashtags`
